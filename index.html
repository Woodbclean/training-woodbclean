Week 1 · Welcome & who we are

Welcome to WoodBClean

Our company story, values, how we conduct ourselves, key handling, and non-solicitation. Everything you need to know before your first job.

Who we are

WoodBClean is a professional cleaning company serving South East Queensland. We offer residential and specialist cleaning services — from everyday house cleans to complex mould remediation and government contracts.

We are not just another cleaning company. We are a team of trained professionals who take pride in the quality of our work and the trust our clients place in us every time they hand over their keys.

Our 4 core values

Integrity
Always honest — with clients, with teammates, whether or not anyone is watching
Excellence
We never cut corners. Leave when the job meets standard, not when the clock says
Respect
For clients homes, privacy, property and every member of our team
Safety first
Your wellbeing matters above all else. No job is worth a shortcut on safety

What these values mean day-to-day

Integrity means if you break something, tell Bonnie immediately. If you are running late, communicate. If a client offers cash on the side, decline and report it. It means being the same person whether or not anyone is watching.

Excellence means you do not leave a job because the clock says it is time — you leave because the job is done to standard. Check your work before you walk out the door. Ask yourself: would I be happy coming home to this?

Respect means treating every client home as if it were your own. No going through drawers, no moving personal items without need, no making unnecessary noise in someone else space.

Safety first means you never rush a chemical mix, never skip PPE because it is uncomfortable, and never push through fatigue to finish faster. A safe team is a productive team.

On-site conduct — what we always do

Knock before entering any roomEven if you know the client is not home. This is professional habit.
Wear your uniform at all times on siteClean, presentable, and identifiable as a WoodBClean professional.
Keep phone use strictly work-relatedPhotos and job communication only. Put your phone away unless needed.
Greet clients warmly and professionallyHi, I am [name] from WoodBClean, we are here to get started.
Report anything unusual to BonnieDamage, hazards, anything that does not seem right — report it, do not ignore it.

What we never do

Bring personal visitors to a job siteNo friends, family, or guests. This is a client private space.
Go through personal belongingsDrawers, cabinets, wardrobes — never. You are there to clean.
Discuss wages or payments with colleagues on siteWhat you earn is a private matter between you and Bonnie.
Take photos of client personal propertyJob documentation photos only. Never photograph personal items or valuables.
Post about clients or jobs on social mediaNo client addresses, property photos, or job details ever shared online.
Make unnecessary noiseLoud music, personal calls on speaker — be mindful. You are a guest in someone home.

When a client is unhappy — what to do

1
Listen without interrupting
Let them speak fully. Do not get defensive. Just listen.
2
Acknowledge — Thank you for letting me know
You do not have to agree with them to acknowledge how they feel.
3
For something small you can fix — fix it immediately
Then check back: Is that better for you?
4
For anything significant — I will pass this to Bonnie who will contact you today
Then call Bonnie immediately. Never make promises without her approval.
5
Never argue — even if you are right
Stay calm, stay professional, escalate. That is what Bonnie is there for.

Why key handling is so important

When a client gives WoodBClean a key, they are placing enormous trust in us. A lost or misused key is not just an inconvenience — it is a serious security breach that can cost the client money, damage our reputation, and create real legal liability. There are no exceptions to these rules.

The key handling process — every single time

1
Sign the key out
Record your full name, date, time, and job address in the key register before taking the key. Every time.
2
Keep the key on your person on site
The key stays in your pocket at all times while on site. Never leave it on a bench, in a door lock, or unattended.
3
Sign the key back in immediately after the job
Return to the register as soon as the job is complete. Record the time.
4
Return key to secure storage
Keys go back to the designated key safe. Never stored in your personal bag, car, or home overnight unless specifically approved by Bonnie.

If a key is lost — 4 steps immediately

1
Stop and retrace your steps thoroughly
Check pockets, kit bag, vehicle, and everywhere on site. Take a full 5 minutes.
2
Call Bonnie immediately — not after the job
Do not wait. Do not finish the job first. Call the moment you believe a key is lost.
3
Do not contact the client yourself
Bonnie handles all client communication in these situations.
4
Complete a written incident report the same day
Your manager will provide the form. Must be submitted the same day.
Real scenario
You finish a job and are driving home. You reach into your pocket and realise you do not have the client key. You are already 10 minutes away.
Correct response: Pull over safely. Call Bonnie immediately — before going back, before doing anything else. Bonnie will advise whether to return or if the key has been found. Complete an incident report the same day regardless of the outcome.

What is non-solicitation?

Non-solicitation means you agree not to approach WoodBClean clients — directly or indirectly — to offer your own cleaning services, or the services of any other cleaning company, either while you work here or for a defined period after you leave.

This applies to both employees and subcontractors. Clients are introduced to you through WoodBClean. Approaching them privately is a breach of trust and of your agreement.

What counts as solicitation — all prohibited

I do private cleans on weekends — here is my numberOffering your own services directly to a WoodBClean client in any form.
My mate runs a cleaning company — I can get you a better dealReferring WoodBClean clients to a competing business.
Leaving your personal card or contact details at a client propertyAny form of self-promotion at a client premises.
Connecting with clients on social media to promote your servicesUsing contact details obtained through work to market to clients personally.
If a client approaches you and asks for your personal contact details to book you privately — politely decline and let Bonnie know. You are not in trouble for reporting it. You are in trouble if you agree to it.

Your role and responsibilities

What your engagement with WoodBClean means in practice — pay, entitlements, obligations, and your rights at work. This week is tailored to your role.

You are a WoodBClean employee

As a WoodBClean employee, you are engaged under an employment agreement. WoodBClean is your employer — you are covered by the Fair Work Act 2009, the National Employment Standards, and Queensland WHS law.

This means WoodBClean has obligations to you — including paying your wages, providing PPE, covering you under WorkCover Queensland for injuries, and providing a safe work environment. You have obligations in return — including following our policies, maintaining quality standards, and conducting yourself professionally at all times.

You are a WoodBClean subcontractor

As a WoodBClean subcontractor, you are engaged as an independent contractor — not an employee. You operate your own business, invoice WoodBClean for your services, and are responsible for your own tax, superannuation, and insurance obligations.

WoodBClean has obligations to you — including paying your invoices within agreed terms, providing a safe work environment, supplying cleaning products for WoodBClean jobs, and giving you reasonable notice of job requirements. You have obligations in return — including maintaining your ABN, meeting WoodBClean quality standards, holding required insurance, and following our safety procedures.
If you are ever unsure whether your arrangement with WoodBClean is correctly classified as contracting, seek independent advice from the ATO (ato.gov.au) or Fair Work Ombudsman (13 13 94). Both have free guidance on employee vs contractor status.

What applies to both roles

Regardless of your engagement type, the following applies to everyone at WoodBClean:

— WoodBClean values and code of conduct
— Key handling and security policies
— Non-solicitation obligations
— All chemical safety, PPE, and SWMS requirements
— Photo documentation and WhatsApp communication policy
— WoodBClean cleaning standards and quality requirements
— Confidentiality of client information
— Privacy Act obligations regarding client data
— Workplace bullying and harassment policy

The standard of your work and your professional conduct are identical whether you are an employee or subcontractor. You represent WoodBClean on every job.

Equal opportunity

Under Queensland and Commonwealth anti-discrimination law, everyone has the right to work without being treated less favourably because of a protected attribute. At WoodBClean we are committed to equal opportunity regardless of race, gender, age, disability, religion, or sexual orientation.

Do not make jokes or comments about any of these attributes. Do not treat a colleague or client differently based on who they are.

If you believe you have been treated unfairly, report it to Bonnie. You can also contact the Queensland Human Rights Commission: 1300 130 670.

Right to disconnect — Fair Work Act 2024

Under changes to the Fair Work Act effective August 2024, employees have the right to reasonably refuse contact from their employer outside of agreed working hours, unless it is an emergency or unreasonable to do so.

At WoodBClean this means: outside your agreed working hours, you are not obligated to respond to non-urgent messages. Bonnie will always indicate when something is genuinely urgent.

WoodBClean has zero tolerance for workplace bullying

Under the Queensland Work Health and Safety Act 2011 and the Fair Work Act 2009, every person has the right to a workplace free from bullying and harassment — whether you are an employee or a subcontractor, and whether the behaviour comes from a manager, a colleague, or a client.

Verbal abuse and humiliationYelling, insulting, or belittling someone — including in front of others or via WhatsApp.

Spreading rumours or gossipSharing false or private information about a colleague — in person or on social media.
Physical intimidation or aggressionAny threatening behaviour or physical aggression.
Sexual harassmentUnwelcome conduct of a sexual nature — illegal under the Sex Discrimination Act 1984. Zero tolerance.
Reasonable management action — like setting performance expectations or addressing conduct — is not bullying, even if it feels uncomfortable.

Right to refuse unsafe work — employees

Under the Queensland Work Health and Safety Act 2011, you have the legal right to stop work if you believe it presents a serious risk to your health or safety — without facing penalties or dismissal for doing so.

If you are asked to work in a situation you genuinely believe is unsafe: tell Bonnie immediately, describe the hazard, and do not re-enter until it is resolved. You cannot be disciplined for a genuine safety refusal.

Independent advice: WorkSafe Queensland — 1300 362 128.

Workers compensation — employees

As a WoodBClean employee, if you are injured at work you are automatically covered by WorkCover Queensland workers compensation.

Report all injuries to Bonnie the same day. Delayed reporting can affect your claim. Seek medical attention and keep all medical certificates and receipts.

WorkCover Queensland: 1300 362 128 | worksafe.qld.gov.au

Workers compensation covers medical costs, rehabilitation, and lost wages. You cannot be dismissed for making a legitimate claim.

Right to refuse unsafe work — subcontractors

The right to refuse unsafe work under Queensland WHS law applies to subcontractors as well as employees. You have the right to stop work if you genuinely believe it presents a serious safety risk — without penalty.

Tell Bonnie immediately and describe the hazard clearly. Do not re-enter until the hazard is resolved. Contact WorkSafe Queensland on 1300 362 128 for independent advice.

Injury protection — subcontractors

As an independent contractor, you are generally not covered by WorkCover Queensland workers compensation in the same way employees are. If you are injured on a WoodBClean site, you may not have automatic cover for lost income or medical costs.

Public liability insurance is required — covers damage or injury to third parties. Income protection insurance is strongly recommended — covers your lost income if you cannot work.

WoodBClean can provide guidance on suitable policies. Speak with Bonnie before commencing work if you do not yet have cover.

If you are injured on site: report to Bonnie immediately, seek medical attention, and keep all documentation.

Heat stress — working in Queensland

Queensland summers are extreme. Heat stress is a real WHS hazard — particularly in properties without air conditioning or on outdoor jobs.

Signs of heat stress: Excessive sweating, dizziness, headache, nausea. Heat stroke (stopping sweating, confusion) — call 000 immediately.

Drink at least 600ml of water per hour in hot conditions. Take breaks in cool areas. Tell Bonnie if you feel unwell. No job is worth your health.

What is a Safety Data Sheet?

A Safety Data Sheet (SDS) is a legal document that comes with every hazardous chemical product. It contains everything you need to know about that product — what is in it, the hazards it carries, how to use it safely, what PPE to wear, and what to do if something goes wrong.

Under Queensland WHS law, you have the right to access the SDS for every product you use, and the responsibility to read it before using a product for the first time.

The 4 SDS sections you must read first

1
Section 1 — Identification
Product name, supplier, emergency contact. Save the emergency number.
2
Section 2 — Hazard identification
What hazards does this product carry? What warning symbols apply? Read this first.
4
Section 4 — First aid measures
What to do for skin contact, eye contact, ingestion, inhalation — specific to this product.
8
Section 8 — Exposure controls and PPE
Exactly what gloves, eye protection, or respiratory protection this product requires.
All 16 sections matter, but 1, 2, 4, and 8 are the ones you need before picking up any product for the first time. SDS for all Clean Like A Pro products: cleanlikeapro.com.au/pages/sds

Spray bottle labelling — non-negotiable rules

Every bottle must be labelled with the product nameNo label — do not use it. Refill and relabel.
Include dilution rate if it is a diluted productSo anyone picking it up knows exactly what is in it at what strength.
Never put a different product in a bottle without relabellingAn unlabelled or mislabelled bottle is a serious safety hazard and is illegal under WHS regulations.
Never mix two products in the same spray bottleEven if they seem compatible — this is how chemical accidents happen.
Mixing the wrong chemicals can produce toxic gases that cause serious injury or death. This is not an exaggeration. These combinations are never acceptable under any circumstances.

Chemical combinations you must NEVER create

Bleach + ammonia = chloramine gasSeverely damages the respiratory system. Bleach and many glass cleaners, floor cleaners, and bathroom sprays contain ammonia. Always check before combining anything with bleach.
Bleach + vinegar or acidic cleaners = chlorine gasHighly toxic. Never mix bleach with any acidic product.

Any two products not designed to be mixedIf it is not on the label — do not mix it. When in doubt, ask Bonnie.
The Clean Like A Pro product range is designed to be used one product at a time, for its designated purpose. Never combine any two products. Each has its own specific use and they are not designed to work together.

PPE — your first line of defence

PPE is not optional. It is both a legal requirement under Queensland WHS law and a condition of your engagement with WoodBClean. The specific PPE required for each task is in the SDS for that product and our SWMS documents.
Employees: WoodBClean provides all standard PPE — gloves, safety glasses, and non-slip footwear. Bonnie will confirm where to collect your PPE before your first job — this may be on site, in your assigned vehicle, or handed to you directly. Replace worn or damaged PPE through Bonnie.
Subcontractors: You are responsible for providing your own standard PPE meeting WoodBClean requirements. Contact Bonnie for guidance on suitable products. For specialist jobs WoodBClean will confirm any additional requirements in advance.

What is a SWMS?

A Safe Work Method Statement (SWMS) is a written document that identifies the high-risk work involved in a task, describes how risks will be controlled, and must be read and signed by workers before the task begins.

At WoodBClean, SWMS are required for high-risk tasks. Your signature on a SWMS is a legal declaration that you have read it, understood it, and will follow it.

When you need a SWMS at WoodBClean

New build and post-construction cleansConstruction sites are high-risk work environments. SWMS required before starting any new build clean.
Level 2 mould remediationWhole-home remediation is classified as hazardous work. SWMS must be signed before entering the site.
Working at height above 2 metresLadders or scaffolding above 2 metres require a SWMS risk assessment.
Any task Bonnie identifies as high-riskIf Bonnie provides a SWMS for a task — read it and sign it before starting.

Your SWMS obligations

1
Read it completely before signing
You are signing to say you have read, understood, and will follow it. That is a legal document. Do not skim and sign.
2
Ask questions if anything is unclear
Ask Bonnie before you sign — never sign a SWMS if you do not fully understand it.
3
Follow it exactly during the task
Deviating from a signed SWMS defeats its entire purpose and removes your legal protection.
4
Never start a SWMS-required task without signing first
This is a legal requirement. There are no exceptions.

Why incident reporting matters

Incident reporting is a legal requirement under Queensland WHS law. Every incident, near miss, or hazard that is reported gives WoodBClean the information needed to prevent it happening again. Reporting is not about blame — it is about improvement. You will never be punished for reporting an incident honestly.

What must be reported

Any injury to yourself or another personCuts, slips, chemical exposure, muscle strains — any physical injury however minor.
Near missesSomething that almost caused an injury but did not. These are early warnings that prevent future incidents.
Property damageAny damage to a client property — no matter how minor. Report immediately before leaving.
Chemical spills or exposuresAny spill of a cleaning product — in a vehicle or on site.
Lost or misplaced keysAll key incidents must be reported the same day.

How to report

1
Call Bonnie immediately — same day for all incidents
For serious incidents — call straight away, even mid-job.
2
Complete a written incident report form
Your manager will provide the form. Include: what happened, where, when, who was involved, and what was done immediately.
3
For injuries requiring medical attention — 000 first
Safety first. Everything else can wait.
Covering up an incident — even a small one — is always worse than reporting it. If something goes wrong and it later comes out that you knew and did not report, the consequences are far more serious than the original incident.

Manual handling — protecting your body

Back strains, shoulder injuries, and wrist problems are the most common injuries in the cleaning industry. Most are preventable. Using a backpack vacuum instead of an upright is one example of WoodBClean actively reducing your manual handling risk.

Safe lifting — every time

1
Plan before you lift — check weight, clear path, know where it is going
2
Stand close, feet shoulder-width apart, stable footing
3
Bend your knees — never your back — keep your spine upright
4
Grip firmly, hold load close to your body, lift by straightening your legs
5
Turn your feet to change direction — never twist your spine while carrying
6
Ask for help or use equipment for anything too heavy to lift safely alone

Repetitive strain injury prevention

Cleaning involves many repetitive movements. RSI affecting wrists, shoulders, and elbows is common over time.

Switch hands periodically when wiping large surfaces. Do not grip spray bottles too tightly. Stretch hands, wrists, and shoulders during breaks. Report any persistent aching or pain to Bonnie early — do not wait until it becomes a serious injury.

Heat stress — Queensland is serious

🌡
Signs of heat stress
Excessive sweating, dizziness, headache, nausea, weakness. If you notice these — stop work, move to shade or a cool area, drink water, tell Bonnie.

Fatigue management

Fatigue affects judgement, reaction times, and the quality of your work — and it affects your safety on the road. If you are too tired to work or drive safely, contact Bonnie before the job. No job is worth an accident.

If you feel drowsy while driving — pull over at the next safe location, take a 15-minute rest, and call Bonnie.

Why WoodBClean uses backpack vacuums

WHS rationale: Traditional upright vacuums require constant bending, pushing and pulling that causes back injuries over time. Backpack vacuums distribute weight evenly across the spine and allow natural movement — this is a deliberate WHS decision, not a preference.

Better clean: You can vacuum ceiling fans, walls, vents, and hard-to-reach areas far more easily. Better mobility around furniture and up stairs. Faster on large jobs.

Backpack vacuum — correct use every time

1
Check the filter before every use
A clogged filter reduces suction, overheats the motor, and blows dust back into the air. Tap or wash the filter if it is dirty. Never use with a blocked filter.
2
Check the bag or canister — empty when two-thirds full
A full bag dramatically reduces suction and makes the motor work harder.
3
Fit it correctly — shoulder straps adjusted so unit sits against your back
A poorly fitted backpack vacuum causes the same strain it is designed to prevent.
4
Use the correct attachment for each surface
Floor brush for hard floors and carpet. Crevice tool for skirting boards and tracks. Brush attachment for ceiling fans, vents, and fly screens.
5
Always work top to bottom — ceiling fans first, floors last
Dust falls downward. Vacuuming floors before fans means doing them twice.
6
Never vacuum wet or liquid debris
Backpack vacuums are dry only. Vacuuming liquid destroys the motor. If there is liquid — mop first.
7
Store correctly after use — upright, filter cleaned, bag checked

Mould identification — Level 1 vs Level 2

L1
Level 1 — surface mould (all trained staff can treat)
White, grey, or light black patches on grout, silicone, tiles, or painted surfaces. Isolated spots or streaks. Surface-level only, not penetrating structure. Contained to one area.
L2
Level 2 — stop immediately and call Bonnie
Dark black or greenish-black patches (especially slimy or fuzzy). Mould covering more than 1 square metre. Mould in multiple rooms. Strong pervasive musty smell throughout. Mould appearing to come from inside walls or ceiling.
When in doubt — stop and call Bonnie. You are never expected to make this call alone. Escalating unnecessarily is far better than under-treating a Level 2 situation.

Where to check on every job

Shower grout and silicone, bathroom ceiling corners, around window frames and sills, behind and under the toilet, laundry walls, underneath sinks, behind furniture against exterior walls.

You find mould in the bathroom, the laundry, and on the bedroom wall behind the bed.
