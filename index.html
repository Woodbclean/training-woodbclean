Who we are

WoodBClean is a professional cleaning company serving South East Queensland — but we are much more than that. We are a support network, a connection, and a community built around one simple belief: when our team members thrive, our clients feel the difference.

You are not just a cleaner. You are a trained professional who people trust to enter their home, their office, or their workplace. That means something. We want every member of this team to feel proud of the work they do and supported in building a genuinely great career.

Why key handling is so important

When a client gives WoodBClean a key, they are placing enormous trust in us. A lost or misused key is not just an inconvenience — it is a serious security breach that can cost the client money, damage our reputation, and create real legal liability. There are no exceptions to these rules.

The key handling process — every single time

1
Sign the key out
Record your full name, date, time, and job address in the key register before taking the key. Every time.
2
Keep the key on your person on site
The key stays in your pocket at all times while on site. Never leave it on a bench, in a door lock, or unattended.
3
Sign the key back in immediately after the job
Return to the register as soon as the job is complete. Record the time.
4
Return key to secure storage
Keys go back to the designated key safe. Never stored in your personal bag, car, or home overnight unless specifically approved by Bonnie.

If a key is lost — 4 steps immediately

1
Stop and retrace your steps thoroughly
Check pockets, kit bag, vehicle, and everywhere on site. Take a full 5 minutes.
2
Call Bonnie immediately — not after the job
Do not wait. Do not finish the job first. Call the moment you believe a key is lost.
3
Do not contact the client yourself
Bonnie handles all client communication in these situations.
4
Complete a written incident report the same day
Your manager will provide the form. Must be submitted the same day.
Real scenario
You finish a job and are driving home. You reach into your pocket and realise you do not have the client key. You are already 10 minutes away.
Correct response: Pull over safely. Call Bonnie immediately — before going back, before doing anything else. Bonnie will advise whether to return or if the key has been found. Complete an incident report the same day regardless of the outcome.

What is non-solicitation?

Non-solicitation means you agree not to approach WoodBClean clients — directly or indirectly — to offer your own cleaning services, or the services of any other cleaning company, either while you work here or for a defined period after you leave.

This applies to both employees and subcontractors. Clients are introduced to you through WoodBClean. Approaching them privately is a breach of trust and of your agreement.

What counts as solicitation — all prohibited

I do private cleans on weekends — here is my numberOffering your own services directly to a WoodBClean client in any form.
My mate runs a cleaning company — I can get you a better dealReferring WoodBClean clients to a competing business.
Leaving your personal card or contact details at a client propertyAny form of self-promotion at a client premises.
Connecting with clients on social media to promote your servicesUsing contact details obtained through work to market to clients personally.
If a client approaches you and asks for your personal contact details to book you privately — politely decline and let Bonnie know. You are not in trouble for reporting it. You are in trouble if you agree to it.

You are a WoodBClean employee

As a WoodBClean employee, you are engaged under an employment agreement. WoodBClean is your employer — you are covered by the Fair Work Act 2009, the National Employment Standards, and Queensland WHS law.

This means WoodBClean has obligations to you — including paying your wages, providing PPE, covering you under WorkCover Queensland for injuries, and providing a safe work environment. You have obligations in return — including following our policies, maintaining quality standards, and conducting yourself professionally at all times.

You are a WoodBClean subcontractor

As a WoodBClean subcontractor, you are engaged as an independent contractor — not an employee. You operate your own business, invoice WoodBClean for your services, and are responsible for your own tax, superannuation, and insurance obligations.

WoodBClean has obligations to you — including paying your invoices within agreed terms, providing a safe work environment, supplying cleaning products for WoodBClean jobs, and giving you reasonable notice of job requirements. You have obligations in return — including maintaining your ABN, meeting WoodBClean quality standards, holding required insurance, and following our safety procedures.
If you are ever unsure whether your arrangement with WoodBClean is correctly classified as contracting, seek independent advice from the ATO (ato.gov.au) or Fair Work Ombudsman (13 13 94). Both have free guidance on employee vs contractor status.

What applies to both roles

Regardless of your engagement type, the following applies to everyone at WoodBClean:

— WoodBClean values and code of conduct
— Key handling and security policies
— Non-solicitation obligations
— All chemical safety, PPE, and SWMS requirements
— Photo documentation and WhatsApp communication policy
— WoodBClean cleaning standards and quality requirements
— Confidentiality of client information
— Privacy Act obligations regarding client data
— Workplace bullying and harassment policy

The standard of your work and your professional conduct are identical whether you are an employee or subcontractor. You represent WoodBClean on every job.

Right to refuse unsafe work — employees

Under the Queensland Work Health and Safety Act 2011, you have the legal right to stop work if you believe it presents a serious risk to your health or safety — without facing penalties or dismissal for doing so.

If you are asked to work in a situation you genuinely believe is unsafe: tell Bonnie immediately, describe the hazard, and do not re-enter until it is resolved. You cannot be disciplined for a genuine safety refusal.

Independent advice: WorkSafe Queensland — 1300 362 128.

Workers compensation — employees

As a WoodBClean employee, if you are injured at work you are automatically covered by WorkCover Queensland workers compensation.

Report all injuries to Bonnie the same day. Delayed reporting can affect your claim. Seek medical attention and keep all medical certificates and receipts.

Workers compensation covers medical costs, rehabilitation, and lost wages. You cannot be dismissed for making a legitimate claim.

Right to refuse unsafe work — subcontractors

The right to refuse unsafe work under Queensland WHS law applies to subcontractors as well as employees. You have the right to stop work if you genuinely believe it presents a serious safety risk — without penalty.

Tell Bonnie immediately and describe the hazard clearly. Do not re-enter until the hazard is resolved. Contact WorkSafe Queensland on 1300 362 128 for independent advice.

Injury protection — subcontractors

As an independent contractor, you are generally not covered by WorkCover Queensland workers compensation in the same way employees are. If you are injured on a WoodBClean site, you may not have automatic cover for lost income or medical costs.

Public liability insurance is required — covers damage or injury to third parties. Income protection insurance is strongly recommended — covers your lost income if you cannot work.

WoodBClean can provide guidance on suitable policies. Speak with Bonnie before commencing work if you do not yet have cover.

If you are injured on site: report to Bonnie immediately, seek medical attention, and keep all documentation.

Heat stress — working in Queensland

Queensland summers are extreme. Heat stress is a real WHS hazard — particularly in properties without air conditioning or on outdoor jobs.

Signs of heat stress: Excessive sweating, dizziness, headache, nausea. Heat stroke (stopping sweating, confusion) — call 000 immediately.

Drink at least 600ml of water per hour in hot conditions. Take breaks in cool areas. Tell Bonnie if you feel unwell. No job is worth your health.

Tax in Australia — how it works for employees

As a WoodBClean employee, your tax is handled automatically. WoodBClean withholds tax from your wages each pay period and sends it to the ATO on your behalf. This is called PAYG withholding (Pay As You Go). You never have to worry about setting money aside for tax — it is already taken care of.

At the end of the financial year (30 June) you lodge a tax return with the ATO. Depending on your income and deductions, you may receive a refund or owe a small amount. Most employees who work part-year or have work-related expenses receive a refund.

What is a TFN declaration?

A Tax File Number (TFN) declaration is a form you complete when you start a new job. It tells WoodBClean your TFN so they can withhold the correct amount of tax from your wages.

Without a TFN declaration, WoodBClean is legally required to withhold tax at the highest rate (47%). With it, you pay only what you actually owe based on your income.

If you do not have a TFN yet — apply free at ato.gov.au. Takes about 10 minutes and is valid for life.

Superannuation — your retirement savings

Superannuation (super) is money set aside for your retirement. Your employer — WoodBClean — is legally required to pay super on top of your wages at the legislated rate (currently 11.5%, rising to 12% by 2025).

This money belongs to you. It goes into a super fund account in your name and grows over time. You generally cannot access it until you retire, but it is genuinely valuable — even small amounts compound significantly over a working life.

You choose your own super fund. If you do not choose one, WoodBClean will pay into a default fund on your behalf — but you can change this at any time.

What is a Safety Data Sheet?

A Safety Data Sheet (SDS) is a legal document that comes with every hazardous chemical product. It contains everything you need to know about that product — what is in it, the hazards it carries, how to use it safely, what PPE to wear, and what to do if something goes wrong.

Under Queensland WHS law, you have the right to access the SDS for every product you use, and the responsibility to read it before using a product for the first time.

The 4 SDS sections you must read first

1
Section 1 — Identification
Product name, supplier, emergency contact. Save the emergency number.
2
Section 2 — Hazard identification
What hazards does this product carry? What warning symbols apply? Read this first.
4
Section 4 — First aid measures
What to do for skin contact, eye contact, ingestion, inhalation — specific to this product.
8
Section 8 — Exposure controls and PPE
Exactly what gloves, eye protection, or respiratory protection this product requires.
All 16 sections matter, but 1, 2, 4, and 8 are the ones you need before picking up any product for the first time.

The 11 things agents and clients actually fail people on

These are not the obvious things. Everyone cleans the toilet and the sink. These are the spots that separate a good clean from a perfect one — and they are exactly what agents and property managers look for first.
1
Window tracks
The single most common fail point. Dirt, dust, and dead insects pack into these channels. Spray, let dwell, use a small brush or old toothbrush, wipe clean. Agents check these every single time.
2
Dishwasher filter
Pull it out, rinse it under hot water, scrub with a brush. Most tenants never do this. Agents and clients open the dishwasher and this is the first thing they check.
3
Rangehood filter
Remove, soak in hot soapy water or degreaser, scrub with a brush, rinse and replace. Grease builds up here invisibly. A clogged rangehood filter is a guaranteed fail.
4
Inside cupboards and drawers
Every shelf, every drawer, wiped out completely. Crumbs, grime, forgotten debris. Agents open every single cupboard. If it is not clean inside, the whole clean fails.
5
Tops of ceiling fans — both sides
The underside is obvious. The top of each blade collects a thick layer of dust that falls when the fan is turned on. Use a damp microfibre cloth over the top of each blade. Do not use a dry cloth — it just moves the dust into the air.
6
Light switches and power points
Every light switch plate, every power point surround — wiped clean with a damp cloth. These accumulate fingerprints, grease, and grime that is very visible at eye level. Never spray directly — spray onto cloth first.
7
Door frame tops
Stand on a step if needed and wipe the top of every door frame. Dust settles here undisturbed for months. If an agent runs their finger across a door frame top and it comes away grey — the clean fails.
8
Exhaust fans
Every bathroom exhaust fan grille — vacuumed first, then wiped with a damp cloth. Dust and lint pack into these grilles. They are directly in the sightline when you look up in a bathroom.
9
Stove drip trays and burner grates
Remove drip trays and burner grates, soak in hot degreaser solution, scrub thoroughly, dry before replacing. Baked-on food residue on these surfaces is a common fail. Do not leave them soaking and forget to replace them.
10
Grout lines
Grout between tiles — especially in bathrooms and kitchens — collects mould and grime. Use a grout cleaner or bathroom product with dwell time. An old toothbrush is the best tool for stubborn grout lines. Agents compare grout to the original condition.
11
Wall marks — especially near light switches and door handles
Scuff marks, fingerprints, and smudges on painted walls. Use a damp cloth with a small amount of all-purpose cleaner. Test in a hidden area first — some paints are not washable. Document any marks that were there before you started.

Level 2 mould remediation — what subcontractors need to know

Level 2 mould remediation is a specialist service for serious contamination — whole rooms, structural penetration, or properties where the mould has spread beyond surface level. As a subcontractor, you may already hold Level 2 certification, or you may be working toward it. This section covers what Level 2 actually involves and how it works within WoodBClean.

What Level 2 remediation covers

1
Initial assessment and containment setup
Full inspection of the affected area. Identify all contaminated surfaces including hidden areas — wall cavities, subfloor, ceiling space. Set up physical containment using plastic sheeting and negative air pressure to prevent spores spreading to unaffected areas of the property.
2
Full PPE — Level 2 standard
Full Tyvek disposable suit (no skin exposure), P3 half-face respirator or powered air purifying respirator (PAPR), chemical-resistant gloves, eye protection, disposable boot covers. All PPE donned before entering the containment zone. Doffed carefully on exit to avoid cross-contamination.
3
HEPA air filtration running throughout
Commercial HEPA air scrubbers running continuously during all work. This captures airborne spores released during the remediation process. Air is vented to outside where possible. Scrubbers run for a minimum period after work stops before containment is removed.
4
Removal of contaminated materials
Any porous materials that cannot be cleaned — plasterboard, insulation, carpet, timber — must be removed and bagged in sealed heavy-duty plastic. Double-bagged. Labelled as contaminated waste. Disposed of through approved channels. Never taken through unaffected areas of the property unsecured.
5
Treatment of structural surfaces
All remaining structural surfaces treated with antifungal biocide product at correct dilution and dwell time. Applied to surfaces beyond the visible mould zone — mould spreads further than it appears. A second application after drying in severe cases.
6
Clearance testing and documentation
Post-remediation clearance is required before containment is removed and the property is handed back. This may involve air quality testing and surface sampling. Full written report provided to the property owner including before/after photos, products used, and certification of completion.

Whole-home remediation — scope and process

When mould has spread through multiple rooms or systems of a property, whole-home remediation is required. This is one of the most demanding and highest-value services in the cleaning industry.
Residents must vacate for the entire duration of whole-home remediation — typically 3-7 days depending on severity. Pets must also leave. Do not begin work until the property is confirmed vacant.
Work zone by zone — contain, remediate, and clear one zone before moving to the next. Never open containment between zones. Contamination spreads instantly through air movement.
Address the moisture source first — mould always comes back if the cause is not fixed. Identify and document the moisture source (leak, condensation, poor ventilation). Inform the property owner in writing before proceeding.
Document everything — photos before, during, and after every stage. Written log of areas treated, products used, dwell times, air readings. This documentation protects you and the client.
Whole-home remediation rates are significantly higher than standard cleaning — reflecting the specialist skills, equipment, PPE, and liability involved. New build cleans

Post-construction cleaning ranges from a single bathroom to a full commercial fit-out. The scope, hazards, process, and standards are completely different at each level. This module covers all of them.

New build cleaning — what it actually means

A new build clean is not a standard clean. Construction leaves behind a completely different type of contamination — silica dust, plaster, grout haze, paint overspray, adhesive residue, concrete splatter, silicone smears, and builder's grime. The goal is not to make a dirty space clean — it is to transform a construction site into a finished, liveable or workable space.

New build cleans are some of the most demanding and highest-value jobs in the cleaning industry. They require patience, the right products, the right process, and an understanding of what "builder's standard" looks like versus "move-in ready."
Bathroom & kitchen
Single wet areas or kitchen fit-outs. Tile haze, silicone, plaster, and builder's dust.
Whole home
Full post-construction clean of a new residential build. Multi-stage, room by room, inside and out.
Commercial & industrial
Offices, retail fit-outs, warehouses, schools, medical centres. Larger scale, different standards, more hazards.
Silica dust is the number one hazard on every new build job. It is invisible, odourless, and causes permanent, irreversible lung damage (silicosis). There is no cure. A P2 respirator is non-negotiable on every new build clean — no exceptions.

Safety requirements — all new build jobs

P2 respirator — must be worn during all dusty work. Not a surgical mask, not a dust mask — a P2 rated respirator. Correctly fitted, covering nose and mouth.
Safety glasses or goggles — construction dust, tile grout haze, and chemical cleaning products are all hazards to eyes. Glasses on before you start.
Chemical-resistant gloves — new build cleaning involves stronger products — tile haze removers, heavy-duty degreasers, concrete dissolver. Gloves appropriate to the chemicals being used.
Steel-capped boots or covered shoes — construction sites have debris, nails, sharp material, and wet concrete. Open-toed shoes are never acceptable on a new build site.
Never dry sweep or dry vacuum silica dust — this launches fine silica particles into the air you breathe. Always wet-suppress dust first, or use a HEPA-rated vacuum. A standard vacuum spreads silica dust — it does not contain it.
Check the site is safe to enter — on new builds, confirm with the builder or site manager that the property is safe and approved for cleaning to begin. Open construction sites can have live electrical, unstable structures, or hazardous materials.

Post-construction bathroom and kitchen clean

Single bathroom or kitchen fit-out cleans are a common entry point for new build work. The space is small but the contamination is concentrated — tile haze across every surface, silicone smears, grout residue, plaster dust, and builder's grime on everything.

Bathroom fit-out — what you are dealing with

1
Tile grout haze
Fresh grout leaves a white hazy film over every tile surface. This must be removed with a pH-appropriate tile haze remover before it fully cures — the longer it is left, the harder it becomes to remove. Work quickly and methodically. Test product in an inconspicuous area first — natural stone requires a different approach.
2
Silicone and sealant smears
Excess silicone around shower screens, bath edges, and wall junctions. Fresh silicone can be carefully scraped with a plastic scraper (never metal on glass or acrylic). Cured silicone requires a silicone remover product. Always work carefully to avoid scratching surfaces.
3
Plaster and paint splatter
Small splatter spots on tiles, fixtures, and glass. Fine plastic scraper or scalpel blade for glass (held at a very low angle). Paint remover for stubborn spots. Never use abrasives on glass or polished surfaces.
4
Fixture and fitting first clean
Tapware, shower heads, toilet suite, towel rails, toilet roll holders — all need their protective film or stickers removed, then polished. Use a soft microfibre — new chrome and brushed nickel scratch easily.
5
Final result standard
Every tile surface streak-free and haze-free. All grout lines clean and visible. Glass and mirrors spotless. All fixtures polished. No construction debris anywhere. Drains cleared. The result should look as though the bathroom has never been used — because it hasn't.

Kitchen fit-out — specific considerations

Splashback tile haze — same process as bathroom tiles. Work quickly before haze cures. Protect bench surfaces with drop cloth while working on splashback.
Cabinetry — wipe all cabinet doors, drawer fronts, and handles. Remove any stickers. Inside all drawers and cupboards — builder often leaves sawdust and debris inside.
Benchtops — identify the surface type first (stone, laminate, timber, Corian) and use the appropriate product. Stone benchtops are often sealed by the builder but confirm before using any acidic product.
Appliances — remove all protective film from ovens, dishwashers, rangehoods, and integrated appliances. Film left on becomes impossible to remove cleanly. Inside of all appliances wiped out.
Never use strong degreasers on new cabinetry paint finishes — new paint is still curing for weeks after application. Harsh chemicals can lift the finish. Use a diluted all-purpose cleaner and soft cloth.

Whole home post-construction clean

A whole home new build clean is a multi-stage job. Construction dust has settled on every surface in every room. Multiple trades have left residue throughout. The property needs to go from construction site to move-in ready — often within a tight timeframe before handover to the owner or tenant.

Whole home new builds can take 1-3 days for a standard residential home depending on size, condition, and number of wet areas. Estimate conservatively — it always takes longer than expected.

Order of attack — whole home new build

1
Builder's inspection first
Walk through the entire property with the builder or site manager before you start. Note any areas of damage, incomplete work, or specific concerns. Document with photos. This protects you — you are not responsible for anything that was already damaged before you arrived.
2
Remove all construction debris and rubbish
Builder is responsible for bulk rubbish removal — but confirm this before you start. Your job begins once the site is clear of major debris. Remove any remaining off-cuts, packaging, and builder's materials before cleaning begins.
3
Top to bottom — ceiling to floor in every room
Start at the ceiling. Cornices, ceiling surfaces, light fittings, exhaust fans. Then walls, window frames, doors and frames, skirting boards. Floors last. Dust falls down — if you clean floors first, you will need to do them again.
4
Wet areas — bathrooms and laundry
All tile haze removal, silicone work, fixture polishing. Each wet area treated as per the bathroom/kitchen process. This is usually the most time-intensive section of a whole home clean.
5
Kitchen
Full kitchen clean including inside all cabinetry, all appliances, benchtops, splashbacks, sink, tapware, rangehood. Remove all protective films.
6
Windows and glass — inside
All interior glass — windows, sliding doors, shower screens, mirrors, glass splashbacks. Streak-free result. Remove any stickers or protective film from glass. Use glass cleaner and a proper window scraper for paint splatter.
7
All floors — vacuum then mop
HEPA vacuum all hard floors first — do not dry sweep silica dust. Then mop with appropriate product for the floor type. Carpet — HEPA vacuum thoroughly. Check for any plaster or paint spots on carpet that need spot treatment.
8
External — garage, alfresco, paths, windows
Garage floor swept and rinsed. Alfresco and entertaining areas. Paths and driveway — pressure wash if required and available. External windows and sliding door tracks. Check gutters for construction debris if accessible.
9
Final walkthrough and handover
Slow, thorough walkthrough of every room. Check corners, behind doors, inside wardrobes, window tracks, skirting boards. Take photos of the completed result. Confirm with the builder or client that the property meets their handover standard.

Commercial and industrial new build cleans

Commercial and industrial post-construction cleans are a different category entirely. The scale is larger, the hazards can be greater, the standards are set by different parties (building owners, tenants, certifiers), and the work often happens under time pressure before a business opens or a lease begins.
Commercial sites may have additional hazards. Heavier construction residue, industrial coatings, epoxy flooring, specialised fit-outs. Always get a full briefing from the builder or project manager before starting any commercial new build clean.

Types of commercial new build clean

🏢
Office fit-out
Partition walls, workstations, glass offices, kitchenette, bathrooms. Heavy dust throughout. Often tight deadline before staff move in. Carpet protection film to be removed from all floors. All glass streak-free — large format glass partitions are a feature, not an afterthought.
🛍️
Retail fit-out
Display cases, shelving, feature walls, lighting fixtures, fitting rooms, service areas. Often high-gloss surfaces that show every mark. Builder frequently installs fixtures close to handover — fresh adhesive, tile work, and paint may still be curing.
🏭
Warehouse / industrial
Concrete floors (often sealed or polished), large open areas, loading docks, amenities blocks. Significant dust load. May require ride-on floor equipment for large floor areas. Confirm equipment requirements before quoting.
🏥
Healthcare / medical centre
Highest standard of all commercial new builds. Clinical-grade finish required. All surfaces must meet infection control standards before any clinical use. Often involves sign-off by an infection control officer. Extra time, extra care, extra documentation.
🏫
School / childcare / gym
High-traffic facilities built to demanding standards. Rubber flooring, commercial carpets, locker rooms, food prep areas, outdoor areas. Often have specific cleaning product requirements due to chemical sensitivities of users.

Key differences on commercial sites

Site induction required — most commercial construction sites require a formal induction before you can work on site. Confirm this with the project manager. Do not arrive on site without knowing what is required.
SWMS required for commercial sites — a Site-Specific Safe Work Method Statement is required for commercial construction sites. WoodBClean will provide this. Read it before you start, sign it, and keep a copy.
Larger equipment may be needed — commercial floor areas may require a ride-on scrubber, pressure washer, or scissor lift for high access. Confirm what equipment is needed and whether it is provided on site or needs to be arranged.
Work in stages if the build is not complete — on large commercial builds, cleaning sometimes begins while construction is still finishing in other areas. Coordinate with the builder to work in completed zones only.
Quoting commercial new builds — commercial new build cleans are quoted separately and are significantly higher than residential. Always contact Bonnie before quoting any commercial post-construction job. Site-specific variables massively affect time and cost.
Commercial and industrial new build cleans are some of the highest-value work available in the cleaning industry. WoodBClean actively pursues these contracts. As a certified, experienced subcontractor, this is a genuine pathway to higher income and more interesting work.

Order of operations — the rule that never changes

On every new build clean, regardless of size — you always work top to bottom, back to front, dry before wet. This is not a preference. It is the only approach that produces a clean result without having to redo work.
1
Dry work first — remove loose dust and debris before any wet cleaning
HEPA vacuum all surfaces before introducing water or cleaning products. Water mixed with construction dust creates a paste that is significantly harder to remove than dry dust. Get the dry dust out first.
2
Ceiling and high surfaces
Cornices, ceiling surfaces, high windows, tops of door frames, exposed beams or steel. Dust settles — work from highest point down.
3
Walls and vertical surfaces
All walls, window reveals, door frames, skirting boards (top and face). Paint splatter, scuff marks, adhesive residue.
4
Fixtures and fittings
Light switches, power points, door handles, tapware, towel rails, appliances. Remove protective films. Polish.
5
Windows and glass
All glass cleaned after walls and frames — not before. Frame cleaning can re-contaminate glass.
6
Floors last — always
Everything above drops onto the floor. Vacuum then mop/scrub when all other surfaces are done. Never clean floors first.

Construction residue — what it is and how to remove it

1
Tile grout haze
White hazy film over tiles from fresh grouting. Use a dedicated tile haze remover — pH matched to the tile type. Acidic remover for standard ceramic and porcelain. pH-neutral for natural stone. Apply, let dwell, scrub with a white pad, rinse thoroughly. Do not let acid-based remover contact grout for extended periods.
2
Plaster and render splatter
Allow to fully dry — do not try to wipe fresh plaster. Once dry, carefully scrape with a plastic scraper. Then clean the residue with warm water and a microfibre. For stubborn dried plaster on glass — a window scraper blade held at a very low angle.
3
Paint overspray and splatter
Fine mist of paint on glass and fixtures from spray painting. On glass — window scraper blade at low angle after softening with warm water. On chrome and fixtures — methylated spirits on a cloth, gently. On painted walls — do not use solvents, use a damp cloth and light pressure.
4
Concrete and screed residue
Concrete haze on tiles, floors, or lower walls. A diluted acid-based cleaner (phosphoric or citric acid) applied with dwell time, then scrubbed and rinsed. Never use on natural stone. Wear gloves and eye protection when handling acid-based cleaners.
5
Adhesive and tape residue
Builders use tape, stickers, and adhesive for protection and marking throughout construction. Remove stickers first. Adhesive residue — eucalyptus oil, lighter fluid, or a dedicated adhesive remover on a cloth. Test on hidden area first. Do not use solvents on fresh paint.
6
Silicone smears and excess
Fresh silicone — plastic scraper, careful. Cured silicone — dedicated silicone remover, let soak, peel away. Never use a metal blade on acrylic, fibreglass, or polished stone. On glass — a sharp blade at very low angle only after the silicone remover has softened it.
When in doubt about a residue type or the right product — contact Bonnie before proceeding. Using the wrong product on a new build surface can cause permanent damage. On a new build, the surfaces are brand new and irreplaceable — one mistake with the wrong chemical can cost thousands.

Module 11 · Floors & surfaces

Floors & surfaces — the complete guide

Every floor type you will encounter in the field — timber, vinyl, polished concrete, natural stone, tiles, and carpet. The wrong product on the wrong surface causes irreversible damage. This module means you will never guess.

Wrong product on wrong surface = immediate callback, potential liability, and a very unhappy client. Five seconds identifying a floor type saves hours of damage control.

Identify before you clean — always

The single most important habit in floor care. Before you apply any product to any floor, identify the surface type. When in doubt — use plain water and a microfibre mop. You cannot damage a floor with water and a clean microfibre mop.

Common floor types you will encounter at WoodBClean jobs:
1
Timber & floating floors
Warm, natural look. Usually has a wood grain texture. Can feel hollow underfoot (floating). Extremely sensitive to moisture and harsh chemicals.
2
Vinyl / LVT (Luxury Vinyl Tile)
Now in 80%+ of new builds. Looks like timber or stone but is plastic. Durable but can be damaged by solvent-based products. Usually has a slight sheen.
3
Polished concrete
Grey, smooth, often reflective. Common in new builds and commercial properties. Acid cleaners will permanently etch the surface.
4
Natural stone — marble, travertine, slate
Often cold to touch, may have visible veining (marble). Very porous, extremely acid-sensitive. One wrong product can cause irreversible etching.
5
Ceramic and porcelain tiles
Most common in bathrooms and kitchens. Durable and versatile. Main challenge is the grout lines, not the tile itself.
6
Carpet
Vacuum only at WoodBClean level. Steam cleaning is a specialist service referred to a carpet professional. Never apply wet product to carpet.

Timber and floating floors

Timber and floating floors are the most common floor type in residential homes — and the most commonly damaged by cleaners who use too much water or the wrong product. Once swelled, warped, or stripped, timber floors cannot be easily restored.
Do: Use a barely damp microfibre mop. Wring out thoroughly — no standing water ever.
Do: Use a pH-neutral timber floor cleaner or Clean Like A Pro multi-surface on a lightly damp mop.
Do: Dry in direction of the boards. Wipe up spills immediately.
Never: Use a wet mop, steam mop, or bucket-and-mop on timber. Water = warping, swelling, mould under boards.
Never: Use vinegar, bleach, ammonia, or citrus-based products. They strip the protective coating permanently.
Never: Use abrasive pads or scrubbers. Scratches timber finish permanently.
The test: If you press your finger to the floor and it comes up wet — your mop is too wet. The floor should dry in under 60 seconds after mopping.

Vinyl and LVT (Luxury Vinyl Tile)

LVT is now the most common flooring in Australian new builds, apartments, and renovated homes. It looks and feels like timber or stone but is actually a multi-layer synthetic product. It is more durable than timber but still has specific needs.
Do: Use a pH-neutral all-purpose cleaner diluted correctly. Clean Like A Pro multi-surface works perfectly.
Do: Damp mop — more water tolerance than timber but not soaking wet.
Do: Vacuum or dry mop first to remove grit before wet mopping. Grit scratches the wear layer.
Never: Use solvent-based products (acetone, turps, petrol-based). Dissolves the wear layer and the adhesive.
Never: Use highly alkaline cleaners or bleach at full concentration. Discolours and degrades the surface over time.
Never: Use abrasive scrubbing pads. LVT wear layer scratches and dulls permanently.
LVT is often mistaken for timber. The difference: timber feels warm and has natural grain variation. LVT feels slightly cooler and has a more uniform, slightly plastic texture. When in doubt — treat it like timber. You cannot over-protect a floor.

Natural stone — marble, travertine, slate

Natural stone is beautiful and expensive — and it is the floor type most frequently damaged by cleaners. The reason: natural stone is acid-sensitive. Even mildly acidic products cause permanent etching that appears as dull spots or white marks that cannot be polished out without professional restoration.
Acidic products that permanently damage natural stone: vinegar, lemon juice, citrus-based cleaners, grout cleaners with acid, bathroom cleaners with acid, some glass cleaners. If a product smells citrus or is labelled "descaler" — keep it off natural stone entirely.
Do: Use only pH-neutral cleaners specifically safe for stone. Check the Clean Like A Pro SDS before applying anything.
Do: Rinse well with clean water after cleaning to remove all product residue.
Do: Dry immediately — water sitting on porous stone causes staining over time.
Never: Use any acidic product — vinegar, citrus, acid-based descalers. One application can cause permanent dull etching.
Never: Use bleach at full concentration on marble or travertine — can cause discolouration.
How to identify natural stone: look for variation in pattern, veining (especially marble), and a slightly cool, heavy feel underfoot. Porcelain can look like marble — porcelain is safe with most cleaners. When unsure — use pH-neutral only and call Bonnie.

Tiles and grout

Tiles are generally forgiving surfaces — but grout is porous, stains easily, and harbours mould and bacteria. The grout between tiles is usually where the real cleaning work happens.
Do: Use a tile and grout cleaner or Clean Like A Pro bathroom cleaner. Allow adequate dwell time on grout (2-3 minutes minimum).
Do: Use a grout brush on discoloured grout — agitation is what removes embedded grime, not more chemical.
Do: Rinse thoroughly. Product residue in grout attracts dirt and dulls tiles.
Never: Use acid-based cleaners on natural stone tiles or grout adjacent to stone — check tile type first.
Never: Use metal scrubbing tools on tiles. Scratches glazed surfaces permanently.

Grout cleaning — step by step

1
Apply product directly to grout lines
Spray or pour Clean Like A Pro bathroom cleaner directly onto grout. Work in small sections.
2
Let it dwell — 2 to 5 minutes
Chemistry does the work. Do not scrub immediately. Let the product break down the grime first.
3
Agitate with grout brush
Short back-and-forth strokes along the grout line. Old toothbrush works for tight corners.
4
Rinse thoroughly
Remove all product and loosened grime. Residue left in porous grout attracts more dirt faster.

Environmental sustainability and green cleaning

Why eco-conscious cleaning matters, what it means in practice, and how WoodBClean commitment to the environment shapes the way we work.

Why green cleaning matters

Conventional cleaning products contain chemicals that can harm waterways, degrade indoor air quality, and pose health risks to the people who use them every day. As professional cleaners, we use more products more frequently than any household — which means our choices have a significantly greater environmental impact.

Eco-friendly cleaning is no longer a niche preference — it is increasingly required by government contracts, expected by commercial clients, and requested by residential clients. WoodBClean commitment to responsible cleaning is both the right thing to do and a genuine business advantage.
Better for clients: Reduced chemical exposure in their home, safer for children and pets, improved indoor air quality.
Better for you: Lower exposure to harsh chemicals during a working career of repeated use. Fewer respiratory and skin issues over time.
Better for WoodBClean: Required for government and institutional contracts. Differentiates WoodBClean from competitors. Attracts conscious clients.

What makes a cleaning product eco-friendly

Not all products labelled "eco" or "green" are equal. Here is what to look for and what it actually means:
1
Biodegradable surfactants
The cleaning agents break down naturally in waterways instead of persisting in the environment. Look for plant-derived surfactants on the ingredient list.
2
No phosphates
Phosphates cause algae blooms in waterways that deplete oxygen and kill aquatic life. Responsible products exclude them entirely.
3
Concentrated formulas
Less packaging waste, lower transport emissions, lower cost per use. One litre of concentrate can replace 10+ ready-to-use bottles.
4
No artificial fragrance or dye
Synthetic fragrances are common allergens and can affect indoor air quality. Unscented or naturally-scented products are better for sensitive clients.
5
Recyclable or refillable packaging
Trigger spray bottles that can be refilled from concentrate dramatically reduce plastic waste over a working year.
Clean Like A Pro products are concentrated, biodegradable, and designed for professional dilution. Using them correctly — at the right ratio — is already an environmentally responsible choice.

Water conservation in cleaning

Professional cleaning uses significantly more water than household cleaning. Small habit changes across a working week add up to thousands of litres saved per year.
Use microfibre over disposable cloths. A quality microfibre cloth cleans effectively with far less water than cotton cloths or paper towels.
Fill mop buckets to the minimum needed. You rarely need a full bucket. Half-fill, wring thoroughly, refill only when water is visibly dirty.
Turn off taps fully when not in use. A dripping tap wastes 9,000+ litres per year. Check client taps are fully off when you leave.
Use spray bottles instead of soaking surfaces. A targeted spray and dwell time outperforms flooding a surface every time — and uses 90% less water and product.
Allow dwell time to do the work. Proper dwell time means less scrubbing, less repeat application, less water, and better results.

Waste reduction and chemical disposal

How we handle products and waste at the end of a job matters — both for the environment and for safety compliance.
1
Never pour chemicals down storm drains
Storm drains flow directly to waterways — not to treatment facilities. Even diluted cleaning products can harm aquatic environments. Dispose of excess product down a household sink or toilet which connects to the sewage system.
2
Wash microfibre cloths — do not dispose
Quality microfibre cloths last 300+ washes. Washing them correctly (cool water, no fabric softener, air dry) makes them last for years. Disposable wipes are single-use plastic waste.
3
Recycle product containers correctly
Rinse empty spray bottles before recycling. Do not mix chemical residue into recycling. Check your local council's guidelines for chemical containers.
4
Use concentrate and refill
Buying Clean Like A Pro concentrate and refilling trigger spray bottles dramatically reduces plastic waste and cost per clean.

Module 13 · Physical wellbeing

Physical wellbeing and long-term career health

Cleaning is physically demanding work. The people who build long, successful careers look after their bodies. This module is WoodBClean saying: we care about you beyond the job.

Your body is your most important tool

A carpenter protects their tools. A chef looks after their knives. As a professional cleaner, your body is your most essential piece of equipment — and the one most people fail to protect.

The physical demands of cleaning — bending, kneeling, overhead work, repetitive wiping, carrying equipment, loading and unloading vehicles — accumulate over time. Small habits practised consistently make the difference between a long, comfortable career and one cut short by preventable injury.
Back injuries are the number one cause of career-ending problems in cleaning. Most are caused by poor lifting technique and repeated awkward bending — both entirely preventable.
Shoulder and wrist injuries from repetitive scrubbing and wiping are the second most common. Technique changes and regular stretching prevent them.
Knee injuries from extended kneeling on hard surfaces are common. Knee pads and correct technique matter.
Report any pain or discomfort to Bonnie early. A small niggle caught early is treated easily. Ignored for months, it becomes a serious injury that sidelines you for weeks. WoodBClean wants you healthy and working for years.

Daily stretch routine — 5 minutes, every day

Do this before starting work and after finishing. Takes 5 minutes. Makes an enormous difference over a working career.
1
Neck rolls — 30 seconds
Slow, gentle half-circles. Left to right. Releases tension from overhead work and driving. Never full circles backwards.
2
Shoulder rolls — 30 seconds
Both shoulders forward 5 times, backward 5 times. Prevents the shoulder tightness that builds from scrubbing and reaching.
3
Wrist circles and finger stretches — 30 seconds
Extend each arm, circle the wrist 5 times each direction. Spread fingers wide, hold 5 seconds. Prevents RSI from spray bottles and scrubbing.
4
Seated or standing forward fold — 30 seconds
Bend forward from the hips, let your upper body hang loose. Knees slightly bent. Decompresses the spine after bending work. Hold, breathe, release slowly.
5
Hip flexor stretch — 30 seconds each side
Step one foot forward into a gentle lunge, lower the back knee toward the floor. Hold. Counteracts the tightness from kneeling and crouching.
6
Calf raises — 20 reps
Hold a wall or bench for balance. Rise onto your toes, lower slowly. Prevents foot and lower leg fatigue from standing on hard floors all day.

Injury prevention — the habits that matter

Lift correctly, every time. Bend at the knees, not the waist. Keep the load close to your body. Never twist while lifting. Applies to equipment bags, vacuum cleaners, and mop buckets.
Use tools that fit you. Mop handles and squeegees should be at shoulder height when extended. If you are bending your back to mop, the handle is too short.
Swap hands regularly. When wiping large surfaces, switch your leading hand. This distributes strain across both sides of your body.
Wear knee pads for floor-level work. Bathroom grout lines, skirting boards, oven interiors. Ten minutes on hard tiles without knee pads adds up across a career.
Take micro-breaks every 45-60 minutes. Stand up straight, roll your shoulders, take 3 deep breaths, have water. This is not laziness — it is injury prevention.
Never push through sharp or sudden pain. Dull muscle fatigue is normal. Sharp, sudden, or persistent pain is your body signalling damage. Stop and report it.

Hydration and nutrition on the job

Physical work in Australian heat depletes your body faster than desk work. Most fatigue, headaches, and reduced concentration experienced by cleaners in the afternoon is dehydration — not tiredness.
Drink water before you feel thirsty
Thirst means you are already mildly dehydrated. Aim for at least 500ml before starting work, and regular sips throughout the day. In summer, significantly more.
Eat before you start
Physical work on an empty stomach leads to fatigue, poor concentration, and reduced energy. A proper meal 1-2 hours before work makes a measurable difference.
Take your break — it is not optional
A 15-20 minute break mid-job to eat, sit, and recharge is not wasted time. It improves your quality of work in the second half of the job significantly.
Heat awareness in summer
Working in unventilated spaces in Queensland summer is a real heat risk. Know the signs of heat exhaustion — headache, dizziness, nausea, heavy sweating — and stop work, seek shade, and hydrate immediately.

Mental health and working alone

Cleaning is often solo work. You spend hours alone in someone else's space, with no colleagues around. For some people this is peaceful and enjoyable. For others — especially on difficult days — it can feel isolating.

WoodBClean wants to be honest about this: working alone has unique mental health considerations that are rarely talked about in any training program. We are talking about it because we care about every person on this team.
Connect with the team. WoodBClean is a community. You are not alone even when working alone. Text Bonnie, check in with another team member. Connection matters.
Music, podcasts, and audiobooks make solo work genuinely enjoyable. Many experienced cleaners say this is one of the best parts of the job — uninterrupted time to listen and think.
Notice when you are not okay. Persistent low mood, dreading work, feeling overwhelmed — these are signals worth paying attention to. They deserve the same attention as a physical injury.
Talk to Bonnie. If something at work is affecting your mental health — whether it is a difficult client, a hard situation, or just a rough patch — Bonnie wants to know. Not to judge. To help.

Leather furniture care

Leather is one of the most common premium furniture materials — and one of the easiest to damage with the wrong product. Leather is a natural material that needs to be cleaned gently and conditioned to stay supple.
Do: Use a dedicated leather cleaner or a very lightly damp microfibre cloth for surface dust and light marks. Wipe in the direction of the grain.
Do: Use a leather conditioner occasionally to prevent cracking and drying — especially in air-conditioned environments.
Do: Blot liquid spills immediately with a clean dry cloth. Do not rub.
Never: Use all-purpose cleaners, bleach, vinegar, alcohol-based products, or any product not specifically designed for leather. Causes permanent drying, cracking, and discolouration.
Never: Saturate leather with moisture. Excess water causes leather to warp, crack, and mould.
Never: Use dark-coloured cloths on light leather. Dye transfer from cloths can stain lighter leather permanently.

Fabric upholstery care

Fabric upholstery varies enormously — from robust microsuede to delicate linen. Before applying any product, check the care label on the furniture if accessible. Look for W (water-based), S (solvent-based), W/S (either), or X (vacuum only).
Do: Vacuum fabric thoroughly with the upholstery attachment before any wet cleaning to remove loose debris and pet hair.
Do: Spot test any product in a hidden area and wait 5 minutes before proceeding.
Do: Blot stains — never rub. Rubbing pushes stains deeper and spreads them.
Never: Machine wash cushion covers without checking the care label. Many shrink or bleed colour.
Never: Over-wet fabric upholstery. Moisture trapped inside cushions causes mould and a persistent musty smell.

Timber furniture care

Timber furniture — tables, chairs, shelving, cabinetry — requires a gentle approach. The finish (lacquer, oil, wax, raw) determines what is safe to use.
Do: Dust first with a dry microfibre cloth, always following the wood grain direction.
Do: For sealed/lacquered timber, a lightly damp cloth with a small amount of pH-neutral cleaner is safe. Dry immediately.
Do: Use a furniture polish or oil appropriate for the finish type for oiled or waxed timber.
Never: Use excess water on timber furniture. Water causes swelling, white water marks, and warping.
Never: Use silicon-based polishes on natural timber — builds up over time and prevents the timber from breathing.

Stainless steel appliances and surfaces

Stainless steel appliances — fridges, dishwashers, rangehoods, ovens — are common in modern kitchens and require specific care to avoid scratching and streaking.
Do: Always wipe in the direction of the grain (the faint brushed lines). Cross-grain wiping causes micro-scratches that build up over time.
Do: Use a microfibre cloth with a small amount of glass cleaner or dedicated stainless steel cleaner for a streak-free finish.
Do: A tiny drop of baby oil or mineral oil on a dry cloth, buffed in the grain direction, gives a premium showroom finish and repels fingerprints.
Never: Use steel wool, abrasive scrubbers, or abrasive powders on stainless steel. Scratches the surface permanently.
Never: Use bleach on stainless steel. Causes pitting and discolouration over time.
